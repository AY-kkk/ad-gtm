# Representative Style Library

Use this library to choose a communication system, not to copy a brand's script, slogan, logo, cast, music, or footage. Each card names the strategic job, visible grammar, production trade-offs, and a variable to test.

## Selection rule

1. Choose one primary style that matches the audience, claim, channel, and available evidence.
2. Add at most one supporting device (for example, documentary texture inside a product demonstration).
3. State what stays fixed across variants: product facts, offer, CTA, duration, and media placement.
4. Mark legal, rights, and claim review as open when the source or asset is not verified.

## Style cards

### `apple-keynote-minimal`

- **Reference:** Apple Events, a product-launch format ([official archive](https://www.apple.com/apple-events/)).
- **Strategic job:** Make a new capability feel clear, inevitable, and premium.
- **Visual grammar:** One subject per frame, controlled camera movement, generous negative space, restrained palette, precise macro details, clean supers.
- **Copy and audio:** One benefit per beat; calm voice or designed silence; let the product action carry the proof.
- **Good fit:** Hardware, software features, high-consideration products, and launches with a demonstrable interaction.
- **Avoid:** Unverified superlatives, dense feature lists, fake interface states, or a luxury tone that the product cannot support.
- **Production constraint:** Lock product geometry and typography; create text and UI in post when generation may distort them.
- **Test variable:** Benefit-first opening versus product-reveal opening.

### `repetition-memory`

- **Reference:** The Brain Platinum TV advertising format, often discussed as a high-frequency, repeated-memory approach. This card describes the mechanism; it does not endorse health claims or verify individual authorship. Contemporary commentary documents both its memorability and audience fatigue ([The Paper](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_16303864), [Jiemian](https://www.jiemian.com/article/2270337.html)).
- **Strategic job:** Make one category, offer, or action easy to recall under low attention.
- **Visual grammar:** A single mnemonic device, short repeated line, strong contrast, simple character or prop, and a consistent end card.
- **Copy and audio:** One short, legally cleared proposition repeated with controlled variation; rhythmic sound that remains intelligible at low volume.
- **Good fit:** Mass-market, low-involvement offers where recall and direct response matter more than nuance.
- **Avoid:** Repeating a weak or unproven claim, medical promises, harassment-level frequency, or confusing irritation with effectiveness.
- **Production constraint:** Cap repetition count, check platform and category rules, and test negative comments and brand lift alongside clicks.
- **Test variable:** One exposure versus repeated line/end card while holding media frequency constant.

### `nike-human-potential`

- **Reference:** Nike, “Find Your Greatness” ([official video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Tz2643pkb6k)).
- **Strategic job:** Turn an ordinary action into an identity and motivation cue.
- **Visual grammar:** Realistic people, purposeful movement, escalating effort, documentary texture, and a late brand reveal.
- **Copy and audio:** Inclusive second person, sparse narration, emotional rise without inventing a testimonial.
- **Good fit:** Fitness, education, creator tools, and products that help users make progress.
- **Avoid:** Borrowing the reference's line, athlete, music, or exact story beat; claiming transformation without evidence.
- **Production constraint:** Cast and motion continuity matter more than visual spectacle; plan safe, repeatable actions.
- **Test variable:** Personal progress framing versus community framing.

### `dove-real-people`

- **Reference:** Dove Real Beauty purpose work ([official pledge](https://www.dove.com/us/en/campaigns/purpose/real-beauty-pledge.html)).
- **Strategic job:** Build trust by showing people and experiences with less polish and more specificity.
- **Visual grammar:** Natural light, direct address, varied bodies and contexts, visible process, modest graphics.
- **Copy and audio:** Participant-owned language only when consented; distinguish scripted demonstration from a real testimonial.
- **Good fit:** Personal care, health-adjacent education, community products, and services where trust is the conversion barrier.
- **Avoid:** Token casting, unverifiable “real user” claims, or using vulnerability as a substitute for product proof.
- **Production constraint:** Consent, release forms, subtitles, and sensitive-data handling are part of the production brief.
- **Test variable:** Direct-to-camera story versus observational use scene.

### `old-spice-absurdity`

- **Strategic job:** Win attention and distinctiveness through a fast, self-aware comic premise.
- **Visual grammar:** Hard cuts, impossible transitions, visual escalation, deliberate artificiality, and a clean product button.
- **Copy and audio:** Compact jokes that land without requiring cultural context; product benefit must survive the joke.
- **Good fit:** Youth audiences, social feeds, deodorant, food, apps, and products with a simple benefit.
- **Avoid:** Humor that obscures the offer, stereotypes, unsafe imitation, or a reference that depends on a protected character.
- **Production constraint:** Storyboard every transition; reserve edit time for timing and captions.
- **Test variable:** First-second visual surprise versus spoken joke.

### `ikea-everyday-proof`

- **Strategic job:** Show a practical improvement in a recognizable home or work moment.
- **Visual grammar:** Before/after contrast, human-scale spaces, functional close-ups, and a price or availability end card.
- **Copy and audio:** Plain language; let the action demonstrate the benefit.
- **Good fit:** Home, productivity, commerce, and utility products with observable use steps.
- **Avoid:** Staged “before” problems, impossible organization, or price claims without a current source.
- **Production constraint:** Keep dimensions, assembly, and interaction faithful to the supplied product.
- **Test variable:** Pain-first opening versus outcome-first opening.

### `red-bull-action`

- **Strategic job:** Associate the product with energy, challenge, and a memorable point of view.
- **Visual grammar:** Kinetic camera, strong silhouettes, environmental scale, rising stakes, and a short brand tag.
- **Copy and audio:** Minimal explanation; use real action evidence or label a dramatization.
- **Good fit:** Sports, travel, gaming, events, and creator platforms.
- **Avoid:** Unsafe stunts, implied sponsorship, or performance guarantees inferred from spectacle.
- **Production constraint:** Motion safety, continuity, and platform-safe crops need explicit checks.
- **Test variable:** Human action lead versus product-action lead.

### `patagonia-proof-and-purpose`

- **Strategic job:** Connect a product decision to a verifiable value or operating practice.
- **Visual grammar:** Field footage, materials and process details, restrained typography, and a specific closing action.
- **Copy and audio:** Name the evidence and its date; separate a company position from a product performance claim.
- **Good fit:** Durable goods, climate or social-impact products, and B2B products with traceable process evidence.
- **Avoid:** Vague purpose language, greenwashing, or unsupported lifecycle numbers.
- **Production constraint:** Source every factual super and keep an evidence ledger.
- **Test variable:** Product proof first versus purpose proof first.

### `muji-quiet-object`

- **Strategic job:** Signal calm, restraint, and considered utility.
- **Visual grammar:** Neutral palette, tactile surfaces, measured cuts, ambient sound, and minimal end card.
- **Copy and audio:** Few words, concrete nouns, no inflated adjectives.
- **Good fit:** Design goods, wellness routines, stationery, and premium utility.
- **Avoid:** Empty minimalism that hides price, function, or a weak CTA.
- **Production constraint:** Lighting and material fidelity carry the message; test compression on subtle textures.
- **Test variable:** Silent product ritual versus soft narration.

## Rights and attribution boundary

References are analysis aids. Do not reproduce protected creative assets or imply endorsement. Verify current brand, regulatory, music, talent, and platform requirements before production. A style match is a hypothesis about communication mechanics; it is not evidence of campaign performance.
